# Project Overview

The course project provides an opportunity to explore a modern computer vision research problem in greater depth. Projects should demonstrate your ability to formulate a problem, review related work, implement and evaluate a solution, analyze results, and communicate your findings professionally.

- **Project Weight:** 350 points (35% of the course grade)
  - Project Team & Topic: 5 points (Group)
  - Project Proposal: 15 points (Group)
  - Midterm Progress Checkpoint: 50 points (Group)
  - Final Project: 250 points (Group)
  - Peer & Self Evaluation: 30 points (Individual)
  - **Total: 350 points**
- Projects must be completed in **groups of 2–3 students.**
- The project does **not** need to produce publishable research. The objective is to conduct a technically sound investigation using modern computer vision methods.
- Students are encouraged to continue strong projects beyond the course by submitting them to research showcases, workshops, conferences, or arXiv.

## 2. Project Proposal (15 points, Group)

Submit a one-page proposal containing:

- [ ] Problem Statement #cv-problem
- [ ] Background and Related Work #cv-background
- [ ] Research Question / Hypothesis #cv-question-hype
- [ ] Proposed Approach #cv-approach
- [ ] Datasets #cv-dataset #needed
- [ ] Evaluation Metrics #cv-eval
- [ ] Expected Challenges #cv-challenges

## 3. Midterm Progress Checkpoint (50 points, Group)

- [ ] Submit a 3–5 minute recorded presentation.
- [ ] Submit a short written progress report accompanying the presentation.
- [ ] Summarize progress completed.
- [ ] Present preliminary experimental results.
- [ ] Discuss challenges encountered.
- [ ] Outline the remaining work.

## 4. Final Project (250 points, Group)

The final submission consists of:

- [ ] **Presentation (Required)**
  - [ ] 8–10 minute presentation, delivered live during the final two lecture sessions.
  - [ ] Present motivation, related work, methodology, experiments, results, limitations, and future work.
  - [ ] Record your live presentation and submit it on Canvas — either upload the video directly or share an online video link — as a record for future reference; grading is based on the live delivery.
- [ ] **Project Repository (Required)**
  - [ ] GitHub repository (public or private).
  - [ ] Include a README with setup instructions.
  - [ ] Provide sufficient information for reproducing experiments.
- [ ] **Project Webpage (Required)**
  - [ ] Create a simple project webpage using GitHub Pages, Google Sites, Adobe Express, or another platform.
  - [ ] Summarize the project and provide links to the presentation and repository.
- [ ] **Research Poster (Optional but Strongly Encouraged)**
  - [ ] A poster is recommended for students planning to participate in the UTSA Graduate Research Showcase, 3MT, or future research presentations.
  - [ ] Students who choose not to prepare a poster will not be penalized.

_Suggested rubric breakdown (250 points total):_

- Technical execution & experiments – 35–40%
- Analysis & evaluation quality – 20–25%
- Presentation – 15–20%
- Repository & reproducibility – 15–20%
- Webpage – 5–10%

## 5. Peer & Self Evaluation (30 points, Individual)

- [ ] Each student submits a confidential evaluation form consisting of:
  - [ ] Peer ratings of teammates' contribution and collaboration throughout the project.
  - [ ] A self-assessment, including the percentage of the final project grade the student believes they personally earned.
  - [ ] Optional comments noting any concerns about team dynamics or contribution.
- [ ] Scores are based on completion and quality of the evaluation submitted, not on the ratings given to teammates or oneself.
- [ ] Peer and self-assessment input may also be used to adjust individual grades within a group project in cases of significantly unequal contribution.

## **Project Expectations**

- Define a clear research question.
- Implement or adapt one or more computer vision methods.
- Compare against at least one appropriate baseline.
- Evaluate using appropriate datasets and quantitative metrics.
- Analyze successes, limitations, and failure cases.
- Present conclusions supported by experimental evidence.

Projects will be evaluated holistically based on technical quality, experimental evaluation, analysis, reproducibility, and the overall presentation.
